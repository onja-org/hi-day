# Workforce Monitoring Platform

A multi-tenant platform that tracks time and activity for organisations and their members.

## Language

### Tenancy & access

**Organisation**:
The tenant root. All data (users, memberships, teams, projects, and eventually time entries) belongs to exactly one Organisation.
_Avoid_: Tenant, company, account — "tenant-scoped" and "organisation-scoped" mean the same thing here, but Organisation is the canonical term.

**Organisation region** (`Organisation.region`, `OrganisationRegion` enum):
An infrastructure/data-residency region the Organisation's data lives in — not a business or legal concept, and not editable by anyone. Currently a single-value enum (`us`) set at creation and never changed; exists as a placeholder for future multi-region infrastructure, not a real choice yet.
_Avoid_: confusing this with **Disclosure region** (below) — same English word, unrelated concepts on different entities: this one is infrastructure-level and org-wide: Disclosure region is a per-Membership jurisdiction used only to pick disclosure text.

**Organisation slug**:
A unique, system-generated identifier for an Organisation, distinct from its display `name`. Two unrelated Organisations can share the same display name ("Acme Inc" is not reserved) — the slug is what's actually unique, and what routing/URLs key off.
_Avoid_: assuming `name` is unique — it explicitly isn't.

**User**:
An individual's account/identity on the platform. Signing up collects the person's name/email *and* their Organisation's founding details (company name, address, optional website) in one form, but creates only the User — no password, no Organisation, no Membership yet. Those Organisation details sit pending on the User record until the person opens the link emailed to them and sets a password: that single act proves they own the email address (there is no separate "verify your email" step) *and* atomically creates the Organisation and their `admin` Membership in it. A User therefore never exists, mid-signup, with a password but no Organisation, or an Organisation but no password — both land together, or neither does.
_Avoid_: Account (ambiguous with Organisation), Member (that's Membership-scoped — a User isn't a "member" of anything until they hold a Membership)

**Membership**:
The join entity linking a User to an Organisation, carrying that user's Role (and optionally their Team) within that specific Organisation. A User can hold multiple Memberships, one per Organisation they belong to.
_Avoid_: UserOrg, OrgUser

**MembershipRole**:
The permission level a Membership holds within its Organisation: `admin`, `manager`, `member`, or `finance`. The permission matrix is now defined for `admin`/`manager`/`member`: an admin sees and manages every active Membership and Activity in the Organisation, including changing any role and deactivating members (the Organisation must always keep at least one active admin — the last one can't be demoted or deactivated); a manager sees only the Memberships and Activity entries of the team(s) they manage (a manager who manages no team sees no one — not other teamless members), and can change role/deactivate for a member of a team they manage, but **admin access is off-limits to managers entirely** — a manager can neither modify a Membership that's currently `admin` nor grant the `admin` role to anyone, no matter whose team it is — and a manager can never modify their own Membership at all, even to demote or deactivate themselves (see Team-scoping below); a member sees only themselves — peers never see each other's Activity entries even on the same Team. `finance` remains explicitly unimplemented — no permission set exists for it, and it's excluded from assignment (`ASSIGNABLE_MEMBERSHIP_ROLES`). The *enforcement mechanism*: backend routes declare `@RequireRole(...)`, checked by `RoleGuard` against the caller's Membership re-fetched from the database on every request (never trusted off the JWT, which carries identity only — see ADR-0002); `RoleGuard` also rejects any caller with no active Membership in the Organisation at all, even on routes that don't require a specific role. The frontend mirrors this with a `RequireRole` component that shows an access-denied message in place, without redirecting, for an authenticated User who simply lacks the role — redirecting is reserved for "not logged in at all" (see ADR-0005). "Owner" is not a distinct *role* — it is a single-user designation on the Organisation (see **Owner** below), held by an `admin`.
_Avoid_: UserRole, Permission, Owner (as a separate role)

**Owner** (`Organisation.ownerUserId`):
The one User — the founding `admin` who registered the Organisation — allowed to start or cancel account deletion. Not a MembershipRole: an owner is still just an `admin`, and other admins have identical powers in every other respect. Deletion routes let any admin through `RoleGuard` and then check `ownerUserId` in the service, so an owner who is later demoted loses access too. Backfilled to the earliest admin Membership for pre-existing Organisations. There is no ownership transfer yet.
_Avoid_: treating "owner" and "admin" as interchangeable for deletion — other admins can neither delete nor restore the account.

**Account deletion** (Organisation):
An owner closes the whole Organisation from Settings. Two modes: **delete now**, or **schedule** for a future date. Either way the owner must type the Organisation name *and* click a link emailed to them — nothing changes until that link is confirmed. State machine: `active` → (`scheduled` →) **suspended** → **purged**, stored as `Organisation.deletionScheduledFor` / `suspendedAt` / `purgeAt`. *Suspended* cuts access immediately: `RoleGuard` refuses every route (except the owner's status/restore ones), running timers are stopped, every DeviceSession is revoked (agents sign out on their next call), and invitations to it stop working. The owner can restore everything until `purgeAt` (`suspendedAt` + `ORGANISATION_DELETION_GRACE_DAYS`, default 30); an hourly job then *purges* it irreversibly. Purge erases every org-scoped row, the org's Wasabi screenshot objects (by key prefix, so orphans go too), and any User who belonged only to this Organisation. Users and shared Devices that another Organisation still uses are kept. Admins are emailed on schedule/suspension and on completion. Audit rows about the org are deleted except the deletion-lifecycle ones, which keep no personal content. See docs/adr/0021-....
_Avoid_: calling suspension "deletion" — nothing is erased until purge, which is why restore is safe. Also avoid assuming the recovery window means the data is still visible: a suspended Organisation is unreadable to everyone, including the owner, until restored.

**Team-scoping** (manager):
What a manager's Membership-list and Activity visibility are limited to. A Membership can belong to zero, one, or many Teams via the `team_memberships(membershipId, teamId, isManager)` join table; a manager's scope is the set of teams where their own `team_memberships` row has `isManager = true`, and they see anyone sharing at least one of those teams (a manager who manages no team sees no one). This replaced the earlier interim single-team model described in docs/adr/0008 (now superseded) — the lookup was deliberately isolated in `getManagerScopeTeamIds()` so the multi-team rewrite touched that one function's implementation, plus a handful of call sites that needed to start eager-loading the `teamMemberships` relation. The same rule governs the Teams list itself: an admin sees every team in the Organisation, but anyone else (manager or member) only sees the team(s) they belong to — which also limits which team a manager's invite-form UI offers for a new invite (not backend-enforced).

**Team**:
A named grouping of Memberships within an Organisation, used to organise time and reports. Sits between Organisation and User in the tenancy hierarchy ("organisation → team → user"). Every Organisation gets one default Team (named "General") created automatically alongside it, and an Organisation must always retain at least one Team — deleting the last remaining one is rejected. A Membership may belong to zero, one, or many Teams, and may be a manager (`isManager`) of any subset of the teams it belongs to. Deleting a Team detaches its members (their Memberships and history are untouched) rather than deleting them. Creating, renaming, and deleting a Team is org `admin`-only. Managing a Team's membership (add/remove members, promote/demote a manager) is open to an org admin or that Team's own manager(s) — mirroring Project membership role's manager/member split. A plain member of a Team has read-only access: they can view the Team (and who's on it) but can't change anything about it or anyone's role.
_Avoid_: Group, Department

**Invitation**:
A pending request for someone to join an Organisation (and optionally a Team) with a given Role, sent by email, before they have a User account or Membership. Becomes a Membership once accepted.
_Avoid_: Invite (fine informally, but Invitation is canonical)

**Employee count**:
A self-reported headcount an admin can edit on the Organisation (e.g. "we grew from 12 to 40"). Informational only — not the same number as the count of Memberships in that Organisation, which is a computed, authoritative figure (used for billing/seats). The two will diverge in practice and both are needed.
_Avoid_: treating employee count and Membership count as interchangeable

**Activity**:
An append-only, user-authored note ("what I'm working on" style) a member submits about themselves — not automated tracking (that's a future "activity matrix" ticket capturing real usage data). Visible to: the author always; an admin for everyone; a manager for their own team (see Team-scoping above). Peers never see each other's entries.
_Avoid_: Time entry (a real, separate entity — see Time tracking below), Activity log (as in automated tracking).

### Work tracking

**Project**:
A unit of work within an Organisation that time is tracked against, scoped by name unique within its Organisation (case-sensitive). Carries a Client, description, and colour as metadata; may be marked billable with a default rate; and has an Archived state. Contains Tasks and, separately, Project memberships.
_Avoid_: assuming rate implies billable, or vice versa — both are independent, optional fields.

**Client**:
A free-text label on a Project (e.g. "Acme Inc"), used for grouping and filtering — not its own record. Two Projects can carry the identical Client text with no relationship enforced between them; renaming a client means editing it on each Project individually. A dedicated Client entity (with its own identity, so a rename or typo doesn't fragment reporting) may replace this later, but doesn't exist yet.
_Avoid_: treating Client as a foreign key or assuming client names are deduplicated anywhere.

**Archived** (Project):
A Project's terminal-but-reversible state (`archivedAt` set). An Archived Project accepts no changes at all — not its own fields, not its Project memberships — until it's unarchived; reports and time-tracking history logged before archiving remain intact and visible throughout.
_Avoid_: implying Archived is a delete — nothing about an Archived Project's history is hidden or removed.

**Project membership**:
Grants an org Membership access to a specific Project — permission to log time against it, unless its role is `viewer` — with an optional per-Membership rate that overrides the Project's default rate when set (falls back to the Project's rate otherwise; a Project with neither has no rate at all), and a Project membership role (`manager` or `member`) controlling what that person can do on *this* Project specifically. There is no Project-level `admin` — only `manager`/`member`/`viewer`, mirroring how a Team has managers and members with no separate admin tier. A Team can also be assigned to a Project (`ProjectTeam`) as an alternative to adding each Membership individually — see **Project team** below.
_Avoid_: confusing this with Membership (org-level) — a Project membership is a separate, per-Project grant layered on top of an existing Membership.

**Project team** (`ProjectTeam`):
Assigns a whole Team to a Project, granting every Membership on that Team project-MEMBER-equivalent access — the same "can log time, can be assigned Tasks" grant a direct Project membership gives, applied to everyone on the Team at once, present and future. It never confers Project membership role `manager`: that stays an explicit, per-Membership Project membership grant, so delegating project management still requires naming a person, not a Team. Managing a Project's Teams (assign/unassign) uses the same guard as managing its Memberships (org admin/manager, or that Project's own manager). Deleting a Team detaches it from every Project it was assigned to (cascades), same as it detaches from Memberships.
_Avoid_: assuming this grants Project membership role `manager` to the Team's own team-managers — it doesn't; see above.

**Project membership role**:
`manager`, `member` or `viewer`, independent of the org-wide MembershipRole. A `viewer` can see the Project (and is the audience for its team reports, once per-project reports exist) but can't log time against it, isn't offered it in the timer's project picker, and can't be assigned its Tasks — making someone a viewer unassigns any Tasks they held. A viewer who also belongs to a Team assigned to the Project still gets that Team's member-equivalent access, since a grant only ever adds access. A Project membership with role `manager` can manage that one Project — edit it, archive/unarchive it, add/remove members, change other members' roles — even when the person's org role is plain `member`; this is what makes it useful as a delegation mechanism rather than just a label. A plain `member` has read-only access: they can see the Project (and any Team) they're assigned to, but cannot change anything about it or anyone's role. Org `admin`/`manager` can always manage every Project regardless of Project membership role — Project membership role only ever *adds* access, never restricts an org admin/manager. Creating a new Project still requires an org `admin`/`manager` role; *listing/viewing* Projects is open to every org role, scoped per-caller (an org admin sees every Project, everyone else sees only Projects — and Teams — they're themselves a member of).
_Avoid_: MembershipRole (that's the org-wide role on Membership — same manager/member-shaped vocabulary, different, independently-set field, scoped to one Project instead of the whole Organisation). Also avoid "Project admin" — it's "Project manager" now, to keep the term consistent with Team's manager/member model and avoid confusion with the org-wide `admin` role.

**Task**:
A unit of work within a Project that time and activity are attributed to. Belongs to exactly one Project for its whole life — it cannot be moved to a different Project. Carries an optional description, hour estimate, due date, and assignee, plus a status (`open` or `closed`, deliberately binary — no board/workflow states). Closing is reversible (a closed Task can be reopened), same spirit as a Project's Archived state. An Archived Project's Tasks are frozen — no create/edit/close/reopen — until it's unarchived.
_Avoid_: Ticket, Item; Subtask (no such concept exists — a Task is the finest-grained unit, it doesn't nest further)

**Task assignee**:
The single Project membership a Task is assigned to — not an org-wide Membership directly, and not a User directly. A Task can only be assigned to someone who already holds a Project membership on that same Project; unassigning (or removing that person from the Project) always leaves the Task simply unassigned, never in an invalid state.
_Avoid_: assuming any org member can be assigned — only existing Project members are eligible.

### Time tracking

**Time entry**:
A single completed or currently-running span of time, attributed to the org Membership it belongs to and (optionally) a Task within a Project the Membership can log time against — the same "org admin/manager, or a Project membership on this specific Project" rule used elsewhere for project-scoped access (see Project membership role), enforced identically for the web console and the desktop agent. `endedAt` unset means a timer is still running; the database enforces at most one running Time entry per Membership at once — starting a second one while one is already running is rejected (not auto-stopped), so hours never move to a different Project/Task without the person explicitly choosing to move them (see the `switch` action). Carries a `source` — `web`/`agent` for a live-started timer, or `manual` for one entered directly with an explicit start and end — and, for agent-started entries, the Device that started it. Separately it carries a `type` (`normal`/`idle`/`resumed`/`manual`): `source` says *where* the time was tracked, `type` says *what kind of time it is*, and an entry the agent tracked can still be idle or resumed. An `idle` entry is a stretch the person was away for, kept as its own entry rather than deleted so the gap stays reviewable; `resumed` is one they chose to keep after an idle warning. Listed entries also carry `activePercentage`, averaged from the Activity samples covering them. A completed entry can be **edited or split by an org admin only** (anyone's, including their own) — manager, plain member and finance can change no entry's recorded details, not even one they logged themselves. **Deleting is deliberately wider**: anyone may delete their *own* entry, and an admin anyone's — withdrawing a mistimed entry of your own isn't the same power as rewriting recorded hours, and the deletion is recorded either way. The two rules reach the frontend as separate `canEdit` and `canDelete` flags per listed entry, so a row offering only Delete is a normal state. Deletion is a real row deletion, not a flag: the entry is gone from every total immediately, and the `time_entry.deleted` AuditLog row (see Auditability) is the only surviving record of it. The App events, URL sessions and Screenshots captured during its span go with it — see **Capture pruning** below. Creating an entry by hand (`source: manual`, web console only) is **admin-only** as well — a plain member logs time by running a timer, never by typing hours in — and an admin may direct a manual entry at another Membership's timesheet, which is how the web console offers to fill an untracked gap in someone's day. The target still needs their own access to the Project: delegation widens who may *write* the entry, never which Projects it can be written against. An entry that hasn't stopped yet — which includes a *paused* one, since pausing deliberately leaves `endedAt` null — can have its Project, Task and start time corrected in place, and can be deleted outright. A **paused** entry can also be given an `endedAt`, which closes it where it already left off: it isn't accruing, so there is nothing a live `stop` would do differently, and its open Pause span is closed at that same instant so the break never becomes worked time. Only a *ticking* entry refuses an end (use `stop`), since ending a live timer that way would leave no record of how it ended. Moving an unstopped entry to another Project takes the time already accrued with it: the entry is re-attributed whole, never split at the edit point — see **Split** below for the deliberate way to divide one. Editing an entry's times permanently deletes the captured activity left outside the new span (see **Capture coverage**), and the new span may not overlap another of that person's entries — touching endpoints are fine, since that is what `switch` produces, but a genuine overlap is rejected. `GET .../time-entries` defaults to the caller's own entries; an admin/manager can pass another Membership's id to view that person's timesheet — a plain member/finance can only ever reach their own. Note that *viewing* is the one place a manager still counts as org-wide: unlike everywhere else in this glossary, a manager's timesheet visibility is not Team-scoped (see Team-scoping), a known divergence rather than a decision.
_Avoid_: assuming an Organisation's `id`/`organisationId` is safe to read off a Device row for an agent request — a shared machine's Device reflects whoever logged in *last*; agent-authenticated requests resolve the organisation (and user) from the DeviceSession the bearer token was actually issued for instead.

**Auditability** (Time entry):
Every manual Time entry, every edit to an existing one, and every deletion is recorded in the AuditLog — `time_entry.created_manually`, `time_entry.updated` (carrying a `changed` map of `{field: {from, to}}` for only the fields that actually changed; a no-op edit writes nothing), `time_entry.deleted` (carrying a free-text `reason` when the deleter gave one — the single-entry dialog insists on one, batch deletion has nowhere to ask per entry),  `time_entry.split` (naming both halves, the cut instant, and the end the original used to have), and a `time_entry.stopped` backdated by idle-discard carrying `reason: 'idle'` and how much time was discarded. `time_entry.deleted` is the one action whose metadata snapshots the *whole* entry (membership, project, task, start, end, source, device) rather than referencing it by id: the row it describes no longer exists, so a bare id would point at nothing. On a delegated manual entry the actor and the subject differ — `actorUserId` is the admin who wrote it, `metadata.membershipId` the person whose timesheet it landed on. The AuditLog itself has no read-side yet (see Idle policy's own note on what isn't built) — the trail exists, but there's no UI to browse it.

**Timesheet filter**:
What the Timesheets page narrows by, all applied together: Member, Project, Time type, Source and Activity level. Values within one filter are alternatives (OR); across filters they all have to hold (AND); and a filter left untouched narrows nothing — which is why an unset panel shows everything rather than nothing. Member is the odd one out: it changes which entries are *fetched*, where the rest narrow the entries already fetched. Applied once for the whole page, so the Daily, Weekly and Calendar views cannot disagree about what is on screen, and the CSV export carries exactly what is shown.

The Project list is the projects the viewer may log time against (`my-projects`), **unioned with any project present in the entries on screen**. Only an **admin** is organisation-wide there; a manager reaches the projects they hold a Project membership on plus those assigned to a Team they belong to, exactly as a plain member does. Being org-wide for *seeing people* does not extend to tracking or filtering time against work they have no part in. That union is what lets the filter reach an archived project: archived ones are excluded from `my-projects`, correctly, since no new time can be tracked against them — but their past entries still sit in the timesheet.
_Avoid_: deriving the Project list from the entries alone — a project with no time logged in the current range would silently vanish from the filter.

**Capture coverage**:
Which Time entry accounts for an App event, URL session or Screenshot. None of them has a foreign key to one — they're attributed to a Membership and a moment — so coverage is *recomputed on every read* by asking which entry spans that moment. This has two consequences that are easy to miss. First, a capture **no entry covers is not shown at all** in the Activity views: capture only runs while a timer runs, so an uncovered capture has nothing to attribute it to, and surfacing it as "Untracked" was noise rather than information. Second, coverage is **not stored**, so a capture the schema still holds may or may not be visible depending on the entries around it.

**Editing** an entry's times, and **deleting** one, both really do remove the App events, URL sessions and Screenshots inside its span, unless another entry still covers them (`CaptureCleanupService`). Deletion is the one action where the intent is unambiguous, and activity recorded for an entry that turned out to be wrong is wrong too. Coverage is recomputed from whatever entries remain, so an overlapping entry's captures survive; a URL session is judged by overlap rather than by its start, so one running past an entry's end survives for the part still covered. Counts land on the `time_entry.deleted` / `time_entry.updated` AuditLog row, and the edit dialog warns that the deletion cannot be undone before it happens. A pruned Screenshot's stored object is deleted *after* the transaction commits — object storage can't roll back, and a stale object is a safer outcome than a missing one.
_Avoid_: assuming a capture belongs to exactly one Time entry — nothing in the schema says so, and overlapping entries can cover the same moment. Also avoid reintroducing an "Untracked" filter: with uncovered captures hidden, it could only ever return nothing.

**Split** (Time entry):
Cutting one completed Time entry into two at a chosen instant, so a span that turned out to cover two different pieces of work can be attributed separately without being deleted and retyped. Admin-only, same rule as editing. The original row survives as the *first* half — it keeps its id, and only its `endedAt` moves back to the cut — so pause rows pointing at it and earlier AuditLog entries about it stay meaningful; the second half is a new row inheriting the same Project, Task, `source` and Device, which is why splitting a tracked span never turns either half into a `manual` one. The cut must fall strictly inside the span: on a boundary one half would have no duration at all. A Pause span straddling the cut is itself divided — closed at the cut on the first half, reopened from it on the second — so neither half reports paused time over minutes it no longer covers. An entry that hasn't stopped can't be split.
_Avoid_: confusing this with the `switch` action, which ends one entry and starts another *going forward*; a split re-cuts time already recorded.

**Known gaps** (Time entry):
Three things a reader might reasonably expect to exist here and which deliberately don't. There is no **approval lifecycle** — a Time entry is never submitted, approved or locked, so edit and delete are unconditional on its age or state. There is no **overlap check** on any write path: two entries for the same Membership may cover the same wall-clock span, whether created manually or produced by an edit, and nothing warns or blocks. And **manager timesheet visibility is org-wide**, not Team-scoped, diverging from the rule this glossary states for Memberships and Activity. Each is a gap, not a design position.

**Idle policy** (Organisation):
An Organisation's chosen response to a Membership going idle mid-timer: `off`, `flag` (the desktop agent prompts the person to keep or discard the idle stretch — the default), or `auto_subtract` (the agent ends the timer at idle-start automatically, with a passive notice, no prompt). Lives on `Organisation.settings` (the same jsonb column `address`/`website` already use) as `idleHandling`/`idleThresholdMinutes`, editable only by an org admin via the existing organisation-settings update. `flag` is deliberately the default a newly-created Organisation gets, not `auto_subtract` — a false positive silently cutting real tracked time is the specific trust failure the epic's own risk notes call out.
_Avoid_: treating "idle detection" as reliably working everywhere it's deployed — it's built on Electron's `powerMonitor.getSystemIdleTime()`, which depends on the X11 XScreenSaver extension and does not reliably report idle time under Wayland Linux sessions (confirmed non-functional in this project's own dev environment); this is the same "Linux best-effort" caveat the desktop agent already carries elsewhere.

### Capture

**Device**:
A paired installation of the desktop agent (or, later, the browser extension or a mobile app) belonging to a User within an Organisation. Marked "managed" when company-controlled versus personal. Pairing itself only ever happens by approving the device from an already-authenticated web session (see docs/adr/0009) — the agent never collects a password directly. Logging out of the web app (or resetting a password) revokes every DeviceSession for that user, not just the browser's own session; a device discovers this the next time it happens to call the backend (there's no push channel), and any timer it had running gets stopped server-side at that moment rather than left open forever.
_Avoid_: Agent (the Agent is the software; a Device is the paired installation/record it produces)

**Window tracking setting** (Organisation):
An org-level on/off switch, `windowTrackingEnabled` (boolean, defaults `true`), gating whether the desktop agent's local window/app sampling loop is *allowed* to run. Lives on `Organisation.settings` (the same jsonb column `address`/`website`/`idleHandling` already use), editable only by an org admin via the existing organisation-settings update. The desktop agent polls `GET /device/capture-settings` to pick up changes — there's no push channel — and re-checks it immediately on login/logout as well, so a signed-out device never samples. This setting being on isn't sufficient on its own: the sampling loop only actually runs while a Time entry is also currently running for that Membership (Hubstaff-style — tracking follows being clocked in, not merely being logged in), independently polled via the same `/device/time-entries/current` a running timer already uses elsewhere.
_Avoid_: assuming this setting alone is enough to start sampling — see the running-timer condition above. Also avoid assuming Linux window tracking has a single source — see **Window source** below for which applications each one can and cannot see.

**Window source** (Linux):
Where the desktop agent learns which window has focus, and therefore which applications window tracking can see at all. On Wayland it reads X11 first — covering every XWayland client, which is most applications — and falls back to AT-SPI2 (`GnomeWindowFocusTracker`) for Wayland-native windows, which X11 reports as no active window. The two are complementary and neither sees everything: AT-SPI only sees applications publishing an accessibility tree, so JetBrains IDEs (Java/Swing, no Access Bridge) are invisible to it; X11 cannot see Wayland-native windows such as GNOME's own apps. macOS and Windows have no such split — their native APIs see every window.
_Avoid_: concluding an application cannot be tracked because it exposes no accessibility information — that rules out only the AT-SPI half. Also avoid reading the window source off `@paymoapp/active-window`: that addon segfaults inside Electron on Wayland and needs glibc 2.38, so X11 is read as a protocol instead (`X11WindowTracker`).

**App event** / **App activity**:
A single foreground-app/window sample (`AppEvent` entity, `app_events` table) uploaded by the desktop agent's window tracking, batched every 60s (`POST /device/app-events`) — see the Window tracking setting entry above for what gates whether sampling happens at all. Attributed to the Membership signed in on the Device at sample time (same rule as Time entry). No raw-event read API exists — only an aggregated "time spent per app" summary for a given day (`GET .../app-events/summary/mine` for the caller's own, `GET .../memberships/:membershipId/app-events/summary` for admin/manager viewing someone else's, same visibility rule as Activity/Time entry), shown in the web console's Activity → Apps tab. "Time spent" is an approximation — each sample is assumed to represent a fixed 5s of foreground time, not a measured duration.
_Avoid_: confusing this with the **Activity** entity above (user-authored "what I'm working on" notes) — they're unrelated concepts that happen to share a nav section (Activity → Notes vs. Activity → Apps) because that's how this ticket was scoped, not because they're the same thing.

**Screenshot setting** (Organisation):
An org-level on/off switch plus two numbers — `screenshotsEnabled` (boolean, defaults `false`), `screenshotFrequencySeconds` (defaults 600 — 10 min), and `screenshotRetentionDays` (defaults 30) — gating and configuring the desktop agent's screenshot capture. Lives on `Organisation.settings` alongside `windowTrackingEnabled`/`idleHandling`, same mechanism and same `GET /device/capture-settings` endpoint the desktop agent already polled for window tracking (see docs/adr/0012-...). Same running-timer condition as Window tracking setting — `screenshotsEnabled` alone isn't sufficient, capture only runs while a Time entry is also currently running for that Membership.
_Avoid_: assuming a change to `screenshotRetentionDays` is retroactive — it only affects screenshots captured after the change (see Screenshot entry below).

**Screenshot**:
A single captured image of a Membership's primary display (`Screenshot` entity, `screenshots` table), uploaded by the desktop agent's screenshot capture — see the Screenshot setting entry above for what gates and paces it. The image bytes never touch the API: the agent requests a presigned URL and uploads the JPEG straight to Wasabi (S3-compatible object storage), then the agent POSTs only the resulting `storageKey` and capture time as metadata (docs/TECH_DESIGN.md §1/§4's "direct-to-storage" design). Attributed to the Membership signed in on the Device at capture time (same rule as AppEvent/Time entry). Each row's `keptUntil` is fixed at capture time from the org's `screenshotRetentionDays` *as it stood then* — an hourly cleanup job deletes both the row and the underlying object once `keptUntil` passes. No raw browsing API beyond per-day listings, same shape as App event (`GET .../screenshots/mine` for the caller's own, `GET .../memberships/:membershipId/screenshots` for admin/manager viewing someone else's, same visibility rule), shown in the web console's Activity → Screenshots tab; each listed screenshot carries its own presigned, time-limited *viewing* URL, never a permanent link.
_Avoid_: assuming lowering retention deletes existing screenshots sooner — see Screenshot setting above.

**Activity tracking setting** (Organisation):
An org-level on/off switch, `activityTrackingEnabled` (boolean, defaults `false`), gating the desktop agent's local keyboard/mouse input-monitoring loop (see docs/adr/0013-...). Lives on `Organisation.settings` alongside `windowTrackingEnabled`/`screenshotsEnabled`, same `GET /device/capture-settings` mechanism and same running-timer condition (tracking follows being clocked in, not merely being logged in). Kept as its own switch rather than folded into `windowTrackingEnabled` — input monitoring is a materially more sensitive capability than reading foreground window titles, and an org may want one without the other.
_Avoid_: assuming this setting alone is enough to start sampling — see the running-timer condition on Window tracking setting above, which applies identically here.

**Activity level** / **ActivitySample**:
A desktop agent's measure of how busy a 10-minute period of tracked time was (`ActivitySample` entity, `activity_samples` table), using Hubstaff's published method (https://support.hubstaff.com/how-are-activity-levels-calculated/): the period is divided into sixty 10-second segments, and `activePercentage` is `activeSegments / 60 * 100`. **"Sample" here means a measurement taken at an interval** — as in `AppEvent`'s `sampledAt`, and the agent's WindowSampler/ScreenshotSampler — never example or test data.

A segment counts as active when the agent's **idle reading** is below the segment length, meaning the last input landed inside it. The original implementation read a global input hook (`uiohook-napi`, see docs/adr/0013-...); deriving it from idle instead needs no native module and records strictly less — how long since the last input, never that input occurred, nor which.

Attributed to the Membership signed in on the Device at sample time (same rule as AppEvent/Screenshot/Time entry). Ingest-only (`POST /device/activity-samples`) — there is no raw-sample read API. The web console reads it only through **Time entry**, which averages the samples covering an entry, weighted by how much of each period falls inside it, and exposes that as the timesheet's Activity column and activity-level filter. An entry no sample covers reports null rather than 0%: "nothing was measured" is a different claim from "they did nothing".
_Avoid_: reading "sample" as seeded or example data — and note the sampling loop only runs while window tracking is on *and* a timer is running, so activity describes tracked time only.
_Avoid_: confusing this with the **Activity** entity above (user-authored notes) or **App event** (foreground window/app samples) — all three are unrelated concepts that happen to share the Activity nav section. Also avoid assuming every screenshot has a matching ActivitySample — activity tracking is a separate on/off switch from screenshots, and the two capture loops aren't guaranteed to phase-align.

**Disclosure region** (`Membership.disclosureRegion`):
An optional jurisdiction code (e.g. country) admin-set on a Membership, used solely to pick which DisclosureVersion (below) applies to that person. Unset means the org's default, region-less disclosure text applies. Not self-reported at signup — an admin assigns/edits it, the same access level as editing a Membership's role.
_Avoid_: "Region" alone — this codebase already has an unrelated **Organisation region** (above); always say "Disclosure region" for this one. Also avoid assuming this is inferred from IP/browser locale, or that it affects anything besides disclosure-text selection.

**DisclosureVersion** (Organisation):
An immutable, published snapshot of the disclosure text shown to a Device's user as a passive notice while capture (window tracking, screenshots, activity sampling) is running on it, scoped to an Organisation and an optional Disclosure region — see docs/adr/0015-... (supersedes docs/adr/0014-...): capture is never blocked on whether this has been shown or acknowledged. An admin can freely edit a draft in place with no version bump; a separate, explicit publish action is what creates the immutable, numbered DisclosureVersion row, which the desktop agent then starts showing in its notice banner. An existing published version's text is never edited in place, since a Consent record (below) points at a specific version and must keep meaning what it meant at the time it was recorded. Resolution for a given Membership only ever considers published versions: the newest one matching their Disclosure region, falling back to the org's newest region-less published version if none matches.
_Avoid_: treating this as a single mutable text field on `Organisation.settings` alongside `idleHandling`/`windowTrackingEnabled`/etc — those are simple toggles with no history; disclosure text needs its own versioned, auditable entity. Also avoid assuming publishing this gates or pauses capture — see docs/adr/0015-....

**Consent record**:
A per-Device acknowledgment that its user was shown a specific DisclosureVersion and chose to click through it, recording who (the Membership), which Device, which DisclosureVersion, and when. Consent is per Device, not per Membership — someone with two Devices acknowledges separately on each, since the notice is shown locally on that specific machine. Purely an audit trail (see docs/adr/0015-...): there is no "declining," and neither answer changes whether capture runs.
_Avoid_: assuming this gates device use or capture in any way — as of docs/adr/0015-... it's informational record-keeping only, a change from the hard-gate design docs/adr/0014-... originally shipped with.
