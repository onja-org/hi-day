# Hi Day

A multi-tenant platform that tracks time and activity for organisations and their members.

## Language

### Tenancy & access

**Organisation**:
The tenant root. All data (users, memberships, teams, projects, and eventually time entries) belongs to exactly one Organisation.
_Avoid_: Tenant, company, account — "tenant-scoped" and "organisation-scoped" mean the same thing here, but Organisation is the canonical term.

**Organisation region** (`Organisation.region`, `OrganisationRegion` enum):
An infrastructure/data-residency region the Organisation's data lives in — not a business or legal concept, and not editable by anyone. Currently a single-value enum (`us`) set at creation and never changed; exists as a placeholder for future multi-region infrastructure, not a real choice yet.
_Avoid_: confusing this with **Disclosure region** (below) — same English word, unrelated concepts on different entities: this one is infrastructure-level and org-wide: Disclosure region is a per-Membership jurisdiction used only to pick disclosure text.

**Organisation slug**:
A unique, system-generated identifier for an Organisation, distinct from its display `name`. Two unrelated Organisations can share the same display name ("Acme Inc" is not reserved) — the slug is what's actually unique, and what routing/URLs key off.
_Avoid_: assuming `name` is unique — it explicitly isn't.

**User**:
An individual's account/identity on the platform. Signing up collects the person's name/email *and* their Organisation's founding details (company name, address, optional website) in one form, but creates only the User — no password, no Organisation, no Membership yet. Those Organisation details sit pending on the User record until the person opens the link emailed to them and sets a password: that single act proves they own the email address (there is no separate "verify your email" step) *and* atomically creates the Organisation and their `admin` Membership in it. A User therefore never exists, mid-signup, with a password but no Organisation, or an Organisation but no password — both land together, or neither does.
_Avoid_: Account (ambiguous with Organisation), Member (that's Membership-scoped — a User isn't a "member" of anything until they hold a Membership)

**Membership**:
The join entity linking a User to an Organisation, carrying that user's Role (and optionally their Team) within that specific Organisation. A User can hold multiple Memberships, one per Organisation they belong to.
_Avoid_: UserOrg, OrgUser

**MembershipRole**:
The permission level a Membership holds within its Organisation: `admin`, `manager`, `member`, or `finance`. The permission matrix is now defined for `admin`/`manager`/`member`: an admin sees and manages every active Membership and Activity in the Organisation, including changing any role and deactivating members (the Organisation must always keep at least one active admin — the last one can't be demoted or deactivated); a manager sees only the Memberships and Activity entries of the team(s) they manage (a manager who manages no team sees no one — not other teamless members), and can change role/deactivate for a member of a team they manage, but **admin access is off-limits to managers entirely** — a manager can neither modify a Membership that's currently `admin` nor grant the `admin` role to anyone, no matter whose team it is — and a manager can never modify their own Membership at all, even to demote or deactivate themselves (see Team-scoping below); a member sees only themselves — peers never see each other's Activity entries even on the same Team. `finance` remains explicitly unimplemented — no permission set exists for it, and it's excluded from assignment (`ASSIGNABLE_MEMBERSHIP_ROLES`). The *enforcement mechanism*: backend routes declare `@RequireRole(...)`, checked by `RoleGuard` against the caller's Membership re-fetched from the database on every request (never trusted off the JWT, which carries identity only — see ADR-0002); `RoleGuard` also rejects any caller with no active Membership in the Organisation at all, even on routes that don't require a specific role. The frontend mirrors this with a `RequireRole` component that shows an access-denied message in place, without redirecting, for an authenticated User who simply lacks the role — redirecting is reserved for "not logged in at all" (see ADR-0005). "Owner" is not a distinct *role* — it is a single-user designation on the Organisation (see **Owner** below), held by an `admin`.
_Avoid_: UserRole, Permission, Owner (as a separate role)

**Owner** (`Organisation.ownerUserId`):
The one User — the founding `admin` who registered the Organisation — allowed to start or cancel account deletion. Not a MembershipRole: an owner is still just an `admin`, and other admins have identical powers in every other respect. Deletion routes let any admin through `RoleGuard` and then check `ownerUserId` in the service, so an owner who is later demoted loses access too. Backfilled to the earliest admin Membership for pre-existing Organisations. There is no ownership transfer yet.
_Avoid_: treating "owner" and "admin" as interchangeable for deletion — other admins can neither delete nor restore the account.

**Subscription** (`Organisation` → `subscriptions`, one row per Organisation):
An Organisation's billing state. Created in the same transaction as the Organisation itself and always starting a **Trial**, because signup asks for no card and so there is nothing else it could start as. Carries a `status` — today only `trialing`; the provider-driven values (active, past due, cancelled) arrive with the payment integration, which is what makes it a status rather than a boolean. Organisations that existed before trials did have **no row at all**: that absence is how "never been on a trial" is spelled, rather than a null date, and both the banner and the reminder job read it as nothing to say. Billing is `admin`-only *at the API*, not merely hidden in the UI — a manager or member asking for it gets a 403, so the rule holds for every future client. All of Settings is already admin-only, so its Billing section inherits the same gate.
The **amounts come from `billing/pricing.ts`**, passed to Stripe inline on each charge rather than named as Stripe Price objects. They used to live in both places, with a start-up check refusing to boot when they disagreed; the check existed only because of the duplication. One Product is still created or found by name, because Stripe has no inline equivalent for one.
_Avoid_: Plan (not a concept yet — there is one price per person, chosen monthly or annually, and no tiers), Account (ambiguous with Organisation), and treating a missing Subscription row as an expired one — they are different states with different answers.

**Billing period** (`monthly` / `annual`):
Which of the two prices an Organisation pays Hi Day Cloud on. **Annual is one yearly charge of $42.00 a seat, not $3.50 charged twelve times** — the "$3.50 per person / month" the pricing page leads with is that yearly amount divided by twelve, and is a *display* figure derived from what is charged rather than the other way round. Getting the direction wrong advertises one number and charges twelve times another. The real saving against monthly is 12.5%; the pricing page's pill rounds it down to "Save 12%".
_Avoid_: Plan (there is one product at one price per person, so there are no tiers to name — only a period), and treating the monthly-equivalent figure as an amount anything is billed at.

**Purchased seats** (`Organisation.purchasedSeats`):
How many seats an admin has bought, set by the counter on Settings → Billing → Subscription. Null until somebody buys any, which is every Organisation on a trial. A **floor, not a cap**: seats are bought ahead of the people who will fill them, so an admin may buy ten for a team of four, and may equally add a fifteenth member to a ten-seat Organisation. Going over is explicitly allowed — nothing blocks the extra members and they track time normally — but it is **not deferred**: each extra seat is charged to the card on file as the person joins (see **Seats are full** below and `docs/adr/0033-...`). What going over produces is a consent an admin ticks in the invite forms before they can send anything, so nobody is charged for a seat they did not knowingly add. Validated as a whole number between 1 and 10,000; past the ceiling it is refused rather than clamped, since a silently corrected typo would authorise a charge nobody read.
Once an Organisation is paying, the **Stripe subscription item's `quantity`** is the figure that is actually invoiced, and the Billing page reads it rather than this column — Stripe owns what it bills for. The column stays authoritative for a trial, which has no Stripe subscription to hold a number, and is the floor the quantity is derived from afterwards.
_Avoid_: calling it a licence count or a limit — nothing is enforced against it, and the only thing it changes is what the next invoice covers. Also avoid assuming it equals **Billed seat** below: they are equal only while nobody has joined past it. And avoid reading it as what Stripe will charge for a paying Organisation — that is the item quantity.

**Billed seat**:
A person the Organisation pays for — or a seat it bought and has not filled. The count on the invoice is **`max(purchasedSeats, active Memberships)`**: buying ahead costs what was bought, and outgrowing the purchase costs what the team actually is. Billing the smaller number would make over-hiring free; billing only the purchase would turn a billing preference into a hiring freeze. Below, "a person the Organisation pays for" describes which Memberships count toward that second figure. The pricing page calls it "anyone who tracks time", which in this product means **every active Membership**: tracking needs no particular role and no Project — a Time entry may carry no `projectId` at all — so there is no active member unable to track. A Project membership role of `viewer` is a per-project grant that only stops time being logged against that one Project; the same person can track against another, so it cannot make anyone unbilled. **Deactivated Memberships are not billed**, a deliberate departure from Hubstaff, which charges for them until they are fully removed: an invoice that bills for someone an admin has switched off reads as a trick. There is no minimum, so a sole admin is one seat rather than the two Hubstaff would charge for.

A free, non-tracking role — the thing Hubstaff calls a project viewer and bills nobody for — does not exist here yet. When it does it will be the one active Membership that is not a billed seat.
A **pending Invitation is not a billed seat**, and is not counted anywhere that says "members": nobody is billed for an invitation, so the figure the over-seats notice reads and the figure the invoice charges are deliberately the same figure. It becomes a seat when it is accepted and a Membership exists.
_Avoid_: "user" or "licence" (a seat is a Membership, and one User can hold several across Organisations), and reading "anyone who tracks time" as usage — someone who could track and didn't is billed, as per-seat pricing normally works. Also avoid reading the count as the member count alone: see the `max` above, which is what Stripe's quantity is set to.

**Advertised price**:
What the marketing and in-app pages quote. The Organisation-facing figure is always derived from the amount actually charged, which the backend holds in the shape a payment provider bills in: an integer of minor units, a currency, and an interval. The frontend keeps a generated copy so the public pricing page needs no backend call — a public page that can fail because an internal API is down is worse than the duplication that removes — and a test fails the build when the two disagree.
_Avoid_: calling the frontend copy a source of prices; it is an output, and editing it by hand is how the page and the invoice stop agreeing.

**Trial** (`Subscription.trialStartedAt` / `trialEndsAt`):
The 14 days the pricing page promises every new Organisation: "14-day free trial, no credit card required." Started automatically when the founding admin sets their password — the same atomic act that creates the Organisation (ADR-0004) — so an Organisation never exists without one. **Every feature is available throughout**, and for a **Grace period** (below) after it ends. Enforcement itself is still a later ticket: nothing is blocked even once the grace runs out, so today the only consequence of expiry is what the banner says.

**Paying ends the trial on the spot**, whatever `trialEndsAt` says: the first successful payment flips `Subscription.status` to `active`, stamps `trialEndedAt` with that day, and starts the paid period from it. Remaining trial days are forfeited, not credited — see `docs/adr/0031-...`. From then on the Organisation is not in a trial by any reading: no countdown, no banner, no reminder emails (the sweep selects `trialing` rows only), and the Billing page shows the subscription instead. `trialStartedAt` / `trialEndsAt` / `trialEndedAt` stay on the row as history and are read by nothing.

Whether an Organisation is *in* a trial is `Subscription.status` — the one flag every surface switches on, so none of them re-derives it from dates and reaches a different answer. Whether a trial has run out *on its own* is still **derived** from `trialEndsAt`, never stored as a status. A stored flag would make correctness depend on a scheduled job having run, so a day's outage would show an admin "12 days left" on a trial that was already over.

**Days left** round *up*, so a running trial always has at least one day left and the final day reads "1 day left" rather than spending 24 hours at "0". This matters beyond display: the reminder milestones are expressed in the same numbers, which is why the last one is `1` and not `0` — a milestone of `0` would only ever be reached after the trial had already ended.

Admins see the days left as a banner on every page, with a button through to Settings → Billing → Subscription; it is not dismissible (it expires on its own, and carries the only route to subscribing) and it stays on after expiry with changed copy, since an admin who simply sees it vanish has no way to understand or act on their state. **Only the owner is emailed** a reminder (three days out, then on the final day) — they are the one admin who can act on it. The `trialReminderSentAtDaysLeft` column records the lowest milestone already sent, which both makes the hourly job idempotent and lets the milestone list grow without a migration.
_Avoid_: calling it a "free plan" or "freemium" — it is time-limited and converts to a paid Subscription, and nothing about it is a tier. Avoid "trial expired" as a stored state (see above), and avoid assuming an Organisation on no trial is an Organisation whose trial ended.

**Grace period** (`GRACE_DAYS`, 7):
Days of continued access after the thing being paid for runs out, before the Organisation loses it. Measured from the end of that thing — `Subscription.trialEndsAt` for a **Trial**, `currentPeriodEnd` for a paid Subscription — so both endings read identically to the person they happen to, and both are **derived**, never stored, for the same reason trial expiry is. Everything keeps working throughout: grace that stopped you working would only be a countdown to a second punishment, so the product is unchanged and only the banner hardens, naming the date access stops so somebody can plan around it. Deliberately **not** stacked on Stripe's own retry window — for a failed renewal Stripe keeps collecting past `currentPeriodEnd`, so the grace overlaps that rather than extending it, and the real effect is on a cancellation running out or a renewal that never came (see `docs/adr/0030-...`). The same number bounds how late a trial reminder may still be sent, kept tied on purpose: a reminder is worth sending for exactly as long as access survives.
_Avoid_: reading it as a second trial — nobody is charged for it and nothing new is unlocked. Avoid describing the end of grace as currently blocking anything: no enforcement exists yet (see **Trial**), so the date the banner names is a statement of intent. And avoid "grace" for Stripe's `past_due` retries, which are Stripe collecting, not us waiting.

**Billing email** (`Organisation.billingEmail`):
Where billing mail goes when it should not be the founder's login email — the accounts-payable address, typically. Null means nobody has set one, and the **Owner**'s own email answers instead, which is what every Organisation did before the column existed. One value serves both senders: our own billing mail, and the address on the Stripe Customer that receives receipts and Stripe's dunning mail. Any `admin` may set it, like the rest of Billing, and it is edited on Settings → Billing → Subscription. Billing mail goes to this address **and** the Owner's, never this one alone: it is typed by hand and never verified, so a transposed character must not be able to swallow the one message saying access is ending. A change is saved locally whether or not Stripe accepts it, then pushed best-effort — Stripe being unreachable must not stop somebody fixing a typo in exactly the address a warning is about to go to.
_Avoid_: calling it the Organisation's "contact email" — nothing else is sent there, and it is not shown to members. Avoid treating it as authoritative on its own (see the Owner rule above), and avoid assuming it is the address Stripe already holds: before this column existed, the Stripe Customer's email was whatever the payer typed into Checkout, known only to Stripe.

**Auto-renew** (`Subscription.cancel_at_period_end`, in Stripe):
Whether Stripe charges the saved card again at the end of the period. Turning it off sets `cancel_at_period_end` on the Stripe subscription; turning it on clears it. Deliberately **not** a cancellation — `subscriptions.cancel` ends a subscription on the spot and is terminal, which would take away a period somebody has already paid for. While it is off, access runs to `currentPeriodEnd` exactly as it would have, and the Billing page says "Ends on <date>" with an offer to put it back.

Renewal state is **never stored here**: `status`, `cancel_at_period_end`, the period end, the item quantity and the card are all read live from Stripe on each request. A copy in our database would be a second answer that goes stale the moment anybody changes a card in Stripe's own dashboard, and the webhook that would have refreshed it is exactly the thing that can be missed (see **Subscription reconciliation**). The only Stripe facts we keep are the ids needed to find the subscription again, plus the trial history.
_Avoid_: "cancelled" for a subscription with auto-renew off — Stripe's `canceled` is a separate, terminal status that cannot be reversed, and the two states offer completely different actions. Avoid reading `stripeStatus` as whether they are a paying customer: that is `status` (see **Trial**).

**Subscription reconciliation**:
Repairing an Organisation that paid while we were not listening. The webhook is the *authority* on a payment, and deliberately not its only *chance*: a delivery that fails past Stripe's three days of retries, an endpoint missing an event, or a developer with no `stripe listen` running all leave somebody charged by Stripe and trialing here — with a countdown banner and reminder emails going to a paying customer. So the Billing page asks Stripe directly before answering, for the one shape that should not exist: a row still saying `trialing` that already carries a Stripe subscription id. Everything else — every real trial, every active subscription — costs no round trip.
_Avoid_: treating it as a sync. It only ever moves `trialing` → `active`, never the reverse, and never invents a subscription that Stripe does not report.

**Seats are full** (`seatsAreFull`):
Every purchased seat is taken, so the next person to join is an extra one. Deliberately **`>=` the purchased count, not `>`** — the question it answers is not "are we already over" but "will the next arrival cost money", and at exactly the purchased count it will. That is what makes it the right trigger for the consent in the invite forms: an agreement obtained after the seat is exceeded is a notification, not consent. False for any Organisation that never bought seats, which is every one on a trial.

_Avoid_: confusing it with **Purchased seats** being exceeded (`isOverPurchasedSeats`, strictly `>`), which is what the Billing page reads. The two differ at exactly one point — a full-but-not-over Organisation — and that point is the only one where consent can still be asked in time.

**Account deletion** (Organisation):
An owner closes the whole Organisation from Settings. Three modes: **delete now** (suspend, with a recovery window), **schedule** for a future date, or **delete permanently now** (no recovery window at all). Either way the owner must type the Organisation name *and* click a link emailed to them — nothing changes until that link is confirmed. State machine: `active` → (`scheduled` →) **suspended** → **purging** → **purged**, stored as `Organisation.deletionScheduledFor` / `suspendedAt` / `purgeAt`; *purging* is not stored but derived — it begins the moment `purgeAt` is reached, and from then on nothing (not even the owner's restore) can stop it. *Suspended* cuts access immediately: `RoleGuard` refuses every route (except the owner's status/restore ones), running timers are stopped, every DeviceSession is revoked (agents sign out on their next call), and invitations to it stop working. The owner can restore everything until `purgeAt` (`suspendedAt` + `ORGANISATION_DELETION_GRACE_DAYS`, default 30); an hourly job then *purges* it irreversibly. Purge erases every org-scoped row, the org's Wasabi screenshot objects (by key prefix, so orphans go too), and any User who belonged only to this Organisation. Users and shared Devices that another Organisation still uses are kept. Admins are emailed on schedule/suspension and on completion. **Delete permanently now** can be chosen from any state short of *purging* — active, scheduled (replacing the schedule) or suspended (ending its recovery window early); it uses the same typed-name-plus-email confirmation, and confirming it puts the Organisation straight into *purging*. If the owner belongs to no other Organisation, their own User is erased by the purge too and they are signed out on confirmation. No "restorable" notice is sent for it — the completion email is the only one. Audit rows about the org are deleted except the deletion-lifecycle ones, which keep no personal content. See docs/adr/0021-organisation-deletion-soft-then-hard.md.
_Avoid_: calling **delete now** "immediate deletion" — only **delete permanently now** erases without a recovery window. Avoid calling suspension "deletion" — nothing is erased until purge, which is why restore is safe. Also avoid assuming the recovery window means the data is still visible: a suspended Organisation is unreadable to everyone, including the owner, until restored.

**Team-scoping** (manager):
What a manager's Membership-list and Activity visibility are limited to. A Membership can belong to zero, one, or many Teams via the `team_memberships(membershipId, teamId, isManager)` join table; a manager's scope is the set of teams where their own `team_memberships` row has `isManager = true`, and they see anyone sharing at least one of those teams (a manager who manages no team sees no one). This replaced the earlier interim single-team model described in docs/adr/0008 (now superseded) — the lookup was deliberately isolated in `getManagerScopeTeamIds()` so the multi-team rewrite touched that one function's implementation, plus a handful of call sites that needed to start eager-loading the `teamMemberships` relation. The same rule governs the Teams list itself: an admin sees every team in the Organisation, but anyone else (manager or member) only sees the team(s) they belong to — which also limits which team a manager's invite-form UI offers for a new invite (not backend-enforced).

**Team**:
A named grouping of Memberships within an Organisation, used to organise time and reports. Sits between Organisation and User in the tenancy hierarchy ("organisation → team → user"). Every Organisation gets one default Team (named "General") created automatically alongside it, and an Organisation must always retain at least one Team — deleting the last remaining one is rejected. A Membership may belong to zero, one, or many Teams, and may be a manager (`isManager`) of any subset of the teams it belongs to. Deleting a Team detaches its members (their Memberships and history are untouched) rather than deleting them. Creating, renaming, and deleting a Team is org `admin`-only. Managing a Team's membership (add/remove members, promote/demote a manager) is open to an org admin or that Team's own manager(s) — mirroring Project membership role's manager/member split. A plain member of a Team has read-only access: they can view the Team (and who's on it) but can't change anything about it or anyone's role.
_Avoid_: Group, Department

**Invitation**:
A pending request for someone to join an Organisation (and optionally a Team) with a given Role, sent by email, before they have a User account or Membership. Becomes a Membership once accepted.
_Avoid_: Invite (fine informally, but Invitation is canonical)

**Employee count**:
A self-reported headcount an admin can edit on the Organisation (e.g. "we grew from 12 to 40"). Informational only — not the same number as the count of Memberships in that Organisation, which is a computed, authoritative figure (used for billing/seats). The two will diverge in practice and both are needed.
_Avoid_: treating employee count and Membership count as interchangeable

**Company profile** (Organisation):
The Organisation's own description of itself, edited by an admin under Settings → Organization → Company information: `name` (required, a real column), plus `industry`, `address`, `taxId` and `website` in the `settings` jsonb blob — all optional, all null until someone fills them in, and all cleared by submitting the field empty. Self-reported metadata: nothing in the product branches on any of them, and no money figure anywhere is labelled with a currency (`settings.currency` is written as `'USD'` when an Organisation is created and read by nothing, so there is deliberately no currency field on the form yet). **Industry** is a closed set (`INDUSTRY_VALUES`) rather than free text, so the values stay groupable; `other` is one of its real values, not an escape hatch that reveals a text box. **Tax ID** is free text with a length cap and no format check — the Organisation carries no country, so there is nothing to validate a VAT/EIN/TIN against.
_Avoid_: treating these as queryable — they live in a jsonb blob, so "every Organisation in Healthcare" is not a query anything can answer today without a migration. Also avoid storing `''` for an empty one: a blank string reads back as "set, but empty", which is why the service normalises it to null.

**Week start** (`settings.weekStartsOn`):
Which day a week begins on for every view that groups time by week — the dashboard's week-to-date figures and day chart, the timesheet's week navigator and calendar grid, the reports' "group by week" and their exported files, and the date pickers' day columns. Sunday or Monday only; Monday by default.

Like the calendar day a Time entry belongs to (see **Viewing timezone**), a week boundary is **computed at read time and never stored**. Changing this therefore re-cuts history: no entry moves and no hours are lost, but which week an entry falls into — and the totals already seen for past weeks — are worked out fresh on the next page load. The Company information form says so next to the two settings that have this property.

Before the setting existed, the frontend was hardcoded to Monday (the timesheet grid, the date pickers, `getWeekRange`) while the backend dashboard services were independently hardcoded to **Sunday**, so a member's own week started on a different day depending on which page they were looking at. Defaulting to Monday settles that split in favour of the convention more of the UI already followed.
_Avoid_: calling date-fns' `startOfWeek`/`endOfWeek` without passing `weekStartsOn` — the app-wide `setDefaultOptions` that used to make Monday the global default is gone, precisely because a global can't vary per Organisation.

**Activity**:
An append-only, user-authored note ("what I'm working on" style) a member submits about themselves — not automated tracking (that's a future "activity matrix" ticket capturing real usage data). Visible to: the author always; an admin for everyone; a manager for their own team (see Team-scoping above). Peers never see each other's entries.
_Avoid_: Time entry (a real, separate entity — see Time tracking below), Activity log (as in automated tracking).

### Work tracking

**Project**:
A unit of work within an Organisation that time is tracked against, scoped by name unique within its Organisation (case-sensitive). Carries a Client, description, and colour as metadata; may be marked billable with a default rate; and has an Archived state. Contains Tasks and, separately, Project memberships.
_Avoid_: assuming rate implies billable, or vice versa — both are independent, optional fields.

**Client**:
A free-text label on a Project (e.g. "Acme Inc"), used for grouping and filtering — not its own record. Two Projects can carry the identical Client text with no relationship enforced between them; renaming a client means editing it on each Project individually. A dedicated Client entity (with its own identity, so a rename or typo doesn't fragment reporting) may replace this later, but doesn't exist yet.
_Avoid_: treating Client as a foreign key or assuming client names are deduplicated anywhere.

**Archived** (Project):
A Project's terminal-but-reversible state (`archivedAt` set). An Archived Project accepts no changes at all — not its own fields, not its Project memberships — until it's unarchived; reports and time-tracking history logged before archiving remain intact and visible throughout.
_Avoid_: implying Archived is a delete — nothing about an Archived Project's history is hidden or removed.

**Project membership**:
Grants an org Membership access to a specific Project — permission to log time against it, unless its role is `viewer` — with an optional per-Membership rate that overrides the Project's default rate when set (falls back to the Project's rate otherwise; a Project with neither has no rate at all), and a Project membership role (`manager` or `member`) controlling what that person can do on *this* Project specifically. There is no Project-level `admin` — only `manager`/`member`/`viewer`, mirroring how a Team has managers and members with no separate admin tier. A Team can also be assigned to a Project (`ProjectTeam`) as an alternative to adding each Membership individually — see **Project team** below.
_Avoid_: confusing this with Membership (org-level) — a Project membership is a separate, per-Project grant layered on top of an existing Membership.

**Project team** (`ProjectTeam`):
Assigns a whole Team to a Project, granting every Membership on that Team project-MEMBER-equivalent access — the same "can log time, can be assigned Tasks" grant a direct Project membership gives, applied to everyone on the Team at once, present and future. It never confers Project membership role `manager`: that stays an explicit, per-Membership Project membership grant, so delegating project management still requires naming a person, not a Team. Managing a Project's Teams (assign/unassign) uses the same guard as managing its Memberships (org admin/manager, or that Project's own manager). Deleting a Team detaches it from every Project it was assigned to (cascades), same as it detaches from Memberships.
_Avoid_: assuming this grants Project membership role `manager` to the Team's own team-managers — it doesn't; see above.

**Project membership role**:
`manager`, `member` or `viewer`, independent of the org-wide MembershipRole. A `viewer` can see the Project (and is the audience for its team reports, once per-project reports exist) but can't log time against it, isn't offered it in the timer's project picker, and can't be assigned its Tasks — making someone a viewer unassigns any Tasks they held. A viewer who also belongs to a Team assigned to the Project still gets that Team's member-equivalent access, since a grant only ever adds access. A Project membership with role `manager` can manage that one Project — edit it, archive/unarchive it, add/remove members, change other members' roles — even when the person's org role is plain `member`; this is what makes it useful as a delegation mechanism rather than just a label. A plain `member` has read-only access: they can see the Project (and any Team) they're assigned to, but cannot change anything about it or anyone's role. Org `admin`/`manager` can always manage every Project regardless of Project membership role — Project membership role only ever *adds* access, never restricts an org admin/manager. Creating a new Project still requires an org `admin`/`manager` role; *listing/viewing* Projects is open to every org role, scoped per-caller (an org admin sees every Project, everyone else sees only Projects — and Teams — they're themselves a member of).
_Avoid_: MembershipRole (that's the org-wide role on Membership — same manager/member-shaped vocabulary, different, independently-set field, scoped to one Project instead of the whole Organisation). Also avoid "Project admin" — it's "Project manager" now, to keep the term consistent with Team's manager/member model and avoid confusion with the org-wide `admin` role.

**Task**:
A unit of work within a Project that time and activity are attributed to. Belongs to exactly one Project for its whole life — it cannot be moved to a different Project. Carries an optional description, hour estimate, due date, and assignee, plus a status (`open` or `closed`, deliberately binary — no board/workflow states). Closing is reversible (a closed Task can be reopened), same spirit as a Project's Archived state. An Archived Project's Tasks are frozen — no create/edit/close/reopen — until it's unarchived.
_Avoid_: Ticket, Item; Subtask (no such concept exists — a Task is the finest-grained unit, it doesn't nest further)

**Task assignee**:
The single Project membership a Task is assigned to — not an org-wide Membership directly, and not a User directly. A Task can only be assigned to someone who already holds a Project membership on that same Project; unassigning (or removing that person from the Project) always leaves the Task simply unassigned, never in an invalid state.
_Avoid_: assuming any org member can be assigned — only existing Project members are eligible.

### Time tracking

**Time entry**:
A single completed or currently-running span of time, attributed to the org Membership it belongs to and (optionally) a Task within a Project the Membership can log time against — the same "org admin/manager, or a Project membership on this specific Project" rule used elsewhere for project-scoped access (see Project membership role), enforced identically for the web console and the desktop agent. `endedAt` unset means a timer is still running. A Time entry is owned by a **Device**, and the database enforces at most one running entry *per (Membership, Device)* — so a person running the agent on a laptop and a desktop has two, started, stopped, paused and resumed independently of each other. The web console has no Device of its own and counts as one: every browser shares a single web timer, and starting, stopping, pausing or resuming there touches only that entry, never a machine's. It may run alongside a Device, but only on the **same Project** — two Devices may differ from each other, since the person chose each one at a time, but a browser disagreeing with a machine is a split they cannot see. Concurrent entries may be on different Projects — see **Worked time** for how a stretch tracked on two of them is credited. Starting a second timer on the *same* Device is rejected (not auto-stopped), so hours never move to a different Project/Task without the person explicitly choosing to move them (see the `switch` action). See **Worked time** for why several running entries do not inflate someone's hours (ADR-0022 superseded ADR-0006's one-entry-per-Membership rule). Carries a `source` — `web`/`agent` for a live-started timer, or `manual` for one entered directly with an explicit start and end — and, for agent-started entries, the Device that started it. Separately it carries a `type` (`normal`/`idle`/`resumed`/`manual`): `source` says *where* the time was tracked, `type` says *what kind of time it is*, and an entry the agent tracked can still be idle or resumed. An `idle` entry is a stretch the person was away for, kept as its own entry rather than deleted so the gap stays reviewable; `resumed` is one they chose to keep after an idle warning. Listed entries also carry `activePercentage`, averaged from the Activity samples covering them. A completed entry can be **edited or split by an org admin only** (anyone's, including their own) — manager, plain member and finance can change no entry's recorded details, not even one they logged themselves. **Deleting is deliberately wider**: anyone may delete their *own* entry, and an admin anyone's — withdrawing a mistimed entry of your own isn't the same power as rewriting recorded hours, and the deletion is recorded either way. The two rules reach the frontend as separate `canEdit` and `canDelete` flags per listed entry, so a row offering only Delete is a normal state. Deletion is a real row deletion, not a flag: the entry is gone from every total immediately, and the `time_entry.deleted` AuditLog row (see Auditability) is the only surviving record of it. The App events, URL sessions and Screenshots captured during its span go with it — see **Capture pruning** below. Creating an entry by hand (`source: manual`, web console only) is **admin-only** as well — a plain member logs time by running a timer, never by typing hours in — and an admin may direct a manual entry at another Membership's timesheet, which is how the web console offers to fill an untracked gap in someone's day. The target still needs their own access to the Project: delegation widens who may *write* the entry, never which Projects it can be written against. An entry that hasn't stopped yet — which includes a *paused* one, since pausing deliberately leaves `endedAt` null — can have its Project, Task and start time corrected in place, and can be deleted outright. Only a *running* entry refuses an end (use `stop`), since ending a live timer that way would leave no record of how it ended. Moving an unstopped entry to another Project takes the time already accrued with it: the entry is re-attributed whole, never split at the edit point — see **Split** below for the deliberate way to divide one. Editing an entry's times permanently deletes the captured activity left outside the new span (see **Capture coverage**), and the new span may not overlap another entry *from the same Device* — touching endpoints are fine, since that is what `switch` produces, but a genuine overlap is rejected. Entries from different Devices may overlap freely; that is the normal state (see **Worked time**). `GET .../time-entries` defaults to the caller's own entries; an admin can pass any Membership's id, and a manager the id of anyone sharing a team they manage (**Team-scoping**, the same rule the Members list and Activity use) — a plain member/finance can only ever reach their own. A manager who manages no team therefore sees only themselves. This used to be the one place a manager counted as org-wide; that divergence is gone.
_Avoid_: assuming an Organisation's `id`/`organisationId` is safe to read off a Device row for an agent request — a shared machine's Device reflects whoever logged in *last*; agent-authenticated requests resolve the organisation (and user) from the DeviceSession the bearer token was actually issued for instead.

**Worked time**:
A Membership's hours over some period: the **union** of their Time entries' active stretches, never the sum. Two Devices tracking 09:00–10:00 is one hour worked, not two, so worked time stays bounded by elapsed wall-clock time however many Devices are in use. "Active" excludes **Pause** spans — and a pause on one Device does not subtract time another Device was still tracking, because the person was working on the machine that kept running. Time overlapping only reduces: an hour tracked twice counts once, while an hour nothing else covers always counts, so forgetting to stop one timer costs exactly the stretch it overlapped and nothing more.

Broken down **per Project**, a stretch tracked on two Projects at once is credited to the one with more time in the period (earliest start breaking a tie) rather than to both, so a breakdown always sums to the worked time above it.
_Avoid_: adding up Time entry durations to get someone's hours — that is precisely the double-count this definition exists to prevent.

**Pause**:
Ends the Time entry, exactly as **stop** does — pressing play afterwards begins a new one. Scoped to one Device: pausing on the laptop does not pause the desktop.

A Pause used to be a *span inside* an entry, with the entry staying open around it. That made an entry's Duration disagree with the Start-End shown beside it — one paused overnight read as ten hours against thirteen minutes of work — and left entries open across midnight for the day-boundary split to carry forward, manufacturing rows that held nothing but a pause. Entries recorded under the old model keep their spans and still net them off; every new one is a single unbroken stretch.

_Avoid_: thinking of a Time entry as something that can contain gaps. It is one continuous worked span, so its duration is simply its end minus its start.


**Auditability** (Time entry):
Every manual Time entry, every edit to an existing one, and every deletion is recorded in the AuditLog — `time_entry.created_manually` (carrying a `reason` when the adder gave one), `time_entry.updated` (carrying a `changed` map of `{field: {from, to}}` for only the fields that actually changed; a no-op edit writes nothing), `time_entry.deleted` (carrying a `reason` when the deleter gave one — see **Reason** below; batch deletion has nowhere to ask per entry),  `time_entry.split` (naming both halves, the cut instant, and the end the original used to have), and a `time_entry.stopped` backdated by idle-discard carrying `reason: 'idle'` and how much time was discarded. `time_entry.deleted` is the one action whose metadata snapshots the *whole* entry (membership, project, task, start, end, source, device) rather than referencing it by id: the row it describes no longer exists, so a bare id would point at nothing. On a delegated manual entry the actor and the subject differ — `actorUserId` is the admin who wrote it, `metadata.membershipId` the person whose timesheet it landed on. The AuditLog itself has no read-side yet (see Idle policy's own note on what isn't built) — the trail exists, but there's no UI to browse it.

**Viewing timezone**:
Which timezone the Timesheets page is *read* in — the viewer's own by default, or the viewed member's, or the Organisation's. It changes display only: no stored entry moves, and the three options exist because those are the people whose day you might be trying to reconstruct, not because a full zone picker would be better.

This introduces a distinction the rest of the schema does not make. A Time entry has one **instant** (`timestamptz`, immutable); the **calendar day it belongs to** is a function of a zone, so it is a view concern, not a property of the entry. The same instant is Monday in Nairobi and Sunday in New York, which is why day grouping, day totals, the week shown, the date-range presets and the CSV export are all resolved in the chosen zone rather than the browser's.

A person's zone is **recorded, not asked for** (`User.timezone`): every page load reports the browser's when it differs, because a profile setting nobody fills in is a setting nobody has. Null until a client has reported one, which is why the selector can say "not recorded yet" and fall back to the viewer's rather than guessing. The Organisation's lives in `settings.timezone` — `UTC` for every Organisation at creation, and now **chosen by an admin** on the Company information form (any IANA zone, the same set the API validates against). One place still does not honour it: the dashboard services compute their days and weeks in the *server's* local time, so a dashboard day can disagree with the same day on the timesheet. Known, deliberately left out of the change that added the picker, and not a design decision to preserve.
_Avoid_: computing a day with local `getFullYear`/`getMonth`/`getDate`, or assuming a day is 24 hours — a day the clocks change is 23 or 25, which is what `startOfDayInZone` exists to get right.

**Timesheet filter**:
What the Timesheets page narrows by, all applied together: Member, Project, Time type, Source and Activity level. Values within one filter are alternatives (OR); across filters they all have to hold (AND); and a filter left untouched narrows nothing — which is why an unset panel shows everything rather than nothing. Member is the odd one out: it changes which entries are *fetched*, where the rest narrow the entries already fetched. Applied once for the whole page, so the Daily, Weekly and Calendar views cannot disagree about what is on screen, and the CSV export carries exactly what is shown.

The Project list is the projects the viewer may log time against (`my-projects`), **unioned with any project present in the entries on screen**. Only an **admin** is organisation-wide there; a manager reaches the projects they hold a Project membership on plus those assigned to a Team they belong to, exactly as a plain member does. Being able to *see someone's timesheet* does not extend to tracking or filtering time against work the viewer has no part in. That union is what lets the filter reach an archived project: archived ones are excluded from `my-projects`, correctly, since no new time can be tracked against them — but their past entries still sit in the timesheet.
_Avoid_: deriving the Project list from the entries alone — a project with no time logged in the current range would silently vanish from the filter.

**Capture coverage**:
Which Time entry accounts for an App event, URL session or Screenshot. None of them has a foreign key to one — they're attributed to a Membership and a moment — so coverage is *recomputed on every read* by asking which entry spans that moment. This has two consequences that are easy to miss. First, a capture **no entry covers is not shown at all** in the Activity views: capture only runs while a timer runs, so an uncovered capture has nothing to attribute it to, and surfacing it as "Untracked" was noise rather than information. Second, coverage is **not stored**, so a capture the schema still holds may or may not be visible depending on the entries around it.

**Editing** an entry's times, and **deleting** one, both really do remove the App events, URL sessions and Screenshots inside its span, unless another entry still covers them (`CaptureCleanupService`). Deletion is the one action where the intent is unambiguous, and activity recorded for an entry that turned out to be wrong is wrong too. Coverage is recomputed from whatever entries remain, so an overlapping entry's captures survive; a URL session is judged by overlap rather than by its start, so one running past an entry's end survives for the part still covered. Counts land on the `time_entry.deleted` / `time_entry.updated` AuditLog row, and the edit dialog warns that the deletion cannot be undone before it happens. A pruned Screenshot's stored object is deleted *after* the transaction commits — object storage can't roll back, and a stale object is a safer outcome than a missing one.
_Avoid_: assuming a capture belongs to exactly one Time entry — nothing in the schema says so, and overlapping entries can cover the same moment. Also avoid reintroducing an "Untracked" filter: with uncovered captures hidden, it could only ever return nothing.

**Split** (Time entry):
Cutting one completed Time entry into two at a chosen instant, so a span that turned out to cover two different pieces of work can be attributed separately without being deleted and retyped. Admin-only, same rule as editing. The original row survives as the *first* half — it keeps its id, and only its `endedAt` moves back to the cut — so pause rows pointing at it and earlier AuditLog entries about it stay meaningful; the second half is a new row inheriting the same Project, Task, `source` and Device, which is why splitting a tracked span never turns either half into a `manual` one. The cut must fall strictly inside the span: on a boundary one half would have no duration at all. A Pause span straddling the cut is itself divided — closed at the cut on the first half, reopened from it on the second — so neither half reports paused time over minutes it no longer covers. An entry that hasn't stopped can't be split.
_Avoid_: confusing this with the `switch` action, which ends one entry and starts another *going forward*; a split re-cuts time already recorded.

**Known gaps** (Time entry):
Two things a reader might reasonably expect to exist here and which deliberately don't. There is no **approval lifecycle** — a Time entry is never submitted, approved or locked, so edit and delete are unconditional on its age or state. There is no **overlap check** on any write path: two entries for the same Membership may cover the same wall-clock span, whether created manually or produced by an edit, and nothing warns or blocks. Each is a gap, not a design position.

**Reason** (Time entry):
Free text a person gives when they add time by hand, edit an existing entry, or delete one — kept on that action's AuditLog row (`metadata.reason`) and shown as the Manual time edits report's `reason` column. It is *text, not a reference*: the Organisation offers a list of **Timesheet reasons** to pick from, but what gets recorded is the wording chosen at that moment, copied onto the audit row, so renaming or deleting a Reason later never rewrites what an existing record says (see docs/adr/0024-timesheet-reasons-stored-as-text.md). A person may always type their own wording instead of picking ("Other…"), and an Organisation with an empty Reasons list gets the plain free-text box. Whether an answer is compulsory is the Organisation's **Require reason** setting (`timesheetRequireReason`, default `true`) — enforced by the API as well as the forms, on adding, editing and deleting time. A batch delete asks once and records that one reason on every entry it removes.
_Avoid_: assuming a blank reason means none was asked for — any action records none when Require reason is off.

**Timesheet reason** (Organisation):
One selectable label in the Organisation's Reasons list ("Forgot to start timer"), managed by an admin under Settings → Activity & tracking → Timesheets → Reasons. Labels are unique within the Organisation, case-insensitively, capped at 50, and three are seeded when an Organisation is created. Nothing references a Timesheet reason — see **Reason** above — so deleting one is a plain row deletion that leaves every past record intact and only changes what the picker offers from then on.
_Avoid_: treating the list as authoritative over history, or expecting a rename to propagate to entries already recorded.

**Timesheets settings** (Organisation):
Three Organisation-level settings covering who may change recorded time: **Modify time** (`timesheetModifyTime`, `add_edit` or `off`), **Require approval** (`timesheetRequireApproval`) — both overridable per Membership, `null` meaning inherit — and **Allow managers to approve their own manual time** (`timesheetManagersApproveOwn`), org-wide with no per-member override because it's a trust boundary rather than a personal preference. An admin clears every per-member override at once with "Apply to all", one server-side UPDATE rather than a PATCH per member.

**Modify time governs managers and members; an admin always may** add manual time, edit and split. The org default is `off`, so out of the box only admins can rewrite hours, and admins aren't listed in the per-member overrides since the setting doesn't apply to them. (This departs from Hubstaff, where the switch binds admins too: an admin locked out of correcting payroll hours by a default they never chose was the worse failure.)

What varies by role is **whose** time, not whether: the grant answers *may you rewrite hours*, and `canSeeMembership` answers *whose*. An `admin` reaches anyone's, a granted `manager` only members sharing a team they manage, a granted `member` only their own. Writing is never wider than reading: the same check guards editing, splitting, and directing a manual entry at someone else's timesheet. Note this makes our `manager` behave like Hubstaff's **Team Lead** (team-scoped) rather than their **Manager** (org-wide) — a deliberate choice, since this product has one manager role where Hubstaff has two. **Deleting is outside this setting entirely** — it stays "your own, or anyone's for an admin" — so a granted manager can edit a team member's entry but not delete it. Deleting is untouched by this: it was already "your own, or anyone's for an admin". The resolved answer for the caller is published as `OrganisationProfile.canModifyTime`, and per entry as the listing's `canEdit`, so no client re-derives it.

**Require approval and Managers approve own remain declared but unenforced**: there is no approval state on a Time entry, no approver and no queue, so they have nowhere to take effect. Settings shows both greyed out as "coming soon", keeping any stored value for when approvals exist.
_Avoid_: reading those two as enforced. **Require reason**, by contrast, is: the `reason` DTO fields are optional in shape, but `TimeEntriesService` refuses a blank one while the setting is on. Splitting an entry isn't a change to recorded hours and asks for no reason.

**Idle policy** (Organisation):
An Organisation's chosen response to a Membership going idle mid-timer: `off`, `flag` (the desktop agent prompts the person to keep or discard the idle stretch — the default), or `auto_subtract` (the agent ends the timer at idle-start automatically, with a passive notice, no prompt). Lives on `Organisation.settings` (the same jsonb column `address`/`website` already use) as `idleHandling`/`idleThresholdMinutes`, editable only by an org admin via the existing organisation-settings update. `flag` is deliberately the default a newly-created Organisation gets, not `auto_subtract` — a false positive silently cutting real tracked time is the specific trust failure the epic's own risk notes call out.
_Avoid_: treating "idle detection" as reliably working everywhere it's deployed — it's built on Electron's `powerMonitor.getSystemIdleTime()`, which depends on the X11 XScreenSaver extension and does not reliably report idle time under Wayland Linux sessions (confirmed non-functional in this project's own dev environment); this is the same "Linux best-effort" caveat the desktop agent already carries elsewhere.

**Offline** (desktop agent):
The state of the agent being unable to *reach* the server — specifically a transport failure, with no HTTP status at all. A refusal carrying a status (a 403 from a work time limit, a 401 from a revoked session) is the server's *answer* and is never offline, however unwelcome: repeating it changes nothing, while an unreached request is always worth retrying. The agent stays usable in this state by reading the last answer it was given (`offline-cache.json` — projects, tasks, capture settings, the idle policy, the running Time entry, the day's totals), queueing start/stop/switch with the instant they were pressed, and replaying them with a stable `opId` so a resend after a lost response cannot write the hours twice. It never *decides* anything it could not have been told: the server stays authoritative, and the cache is memory, not judgement. **There is no limit on how long a device may track offline** — somebody genuinely away from a connection is who this is for — but capture (screenshots, window and URL tracking) stops after seven days out of touch, with a notice, since those settings say whether a person's screen may be photographed and an admin who revokes that is owed an upper bound. Logging in is the one thing with no offline form, since pairing is a conversation with the server. See `docs/adr/0025-...`.
_Avoid_: calling a device "offline" because the server has not heard from it — the Devices page's own active/offline status is about missed heartbeats, a different question from whether the agent can work. Also avoid treating an entry the abandoned-timer sweeper closed as a stopped one: it carries `closedAsAbandoned`, marking the end as the server's *guess* at where the person stopped, which a reconnecting Device may correct with the real stop.

### Capture

**Device**:
A paired installation of the desktop agent (or, later, the browser extension or a mobile app) belonging to a User within an Organisation. Marked "managed" when company-controlled versus personal. Pairing itself only ever happens by approving the device from an already-authenticated web session (see docs/adr/0009) — the agent never collects a password directly. Logging out of the web app (or resetting a password) revokes every DeviceSession for that user, not just the browser's own session; a device discovers this the next time it happens to call the backend (there's no push channel), and any timer it had running gets stopped server-side at that moment rather than left open forever. The Devices page follows Team-scoping: an admin sees and can remotely sign out every Device in the Organisation, a manager only those of people they can see (their own included), and never an admin's — the list shows it, but the sign-out is refused.
_Avoid_: Agent (the Agent is the software; a Device is the paired installation/record it produces)

**Track apps & URLs** (`settings.appUrlTracking`):
The Organisation's 3-way default for what the desktop agent records while a timer runs — `off` (neither), `apps` (the application in use), or `apps_urls` (that plus the URL inside a browser) — overridable per Membership on `Membership.appUrlTracking`, `null` meaning inherit. The agent receives the *resolved* value: `GET /device/capture-settings` applies the caller's own override on top of the org default, keyed off the `DeviceSession`'s `userId` rather than the Device row (see **Time entry**'s _Avoid_ for why that distinction matters on a shared machine). `off` stops the window sampler outright; `apps` runs it; only `apps_urls` additionally starts URL capture, whose lifecycle therefore follows this setting rather than login.

This is the **only** setting deciding whether apps are captured — **Window tracking** no longer qualifies it, it merely strips the title from each sample (see below). Neither gates the activity percentage, which measures how busy tracked time was: that is neither an app, a URL nor a window title, so it rides `timerRunning` alone and survives both being switched off.
Enforced on **both** sides, because agent versions in the wild lag a settings change: the agent stops capturing, and `AppEventsService.recordBatch` / `UrlSessionsService.sync` independently re-resolve the setting and discard anything they shouldn't have been sent. The server drops silently (HTTP 2xx) rather than erroring — a client that has been told "off" has nothing to fix by retrying — and the URL sync *acks* the ids it discards, since an empty ack would leave the batch pending on the device and retried forever.
_Avoid_: expecting a change to land instantly — the agent polls every 15s (`DEFAULT_CAPTURE_SETTINGS_POLL_INTERVAL_MS`, matching `TimerStatusGate` so a change and the stopped timer it causes arrive together) and there is no push channel. Also avoid assuming turning it off erases what is already on the device: sessions captured while it was allowed but not yet synced stay in the agent's local SQLite indefinitely (`UrlSessionStore.purgeOlderThan` exists but nothing calls it), and upload if it is ever switched back on.

**Allowed apps** (`settings.allowedApps`):
Which timer clients a Membership may track from — `all`, `desktop_only` or `mobile_only` — the Organisation default with a per-Membership override on `Membership.allowedApps` (`null` inherits), cleared in bulk by "Apply to all" like **Track apps & URLs**.

Enforced twice, both through `isTimerSourceAllowed` so they cannot disagree: `TimeEntriesService.start` and `switchTo` (which starts a timer too) refuse a `source` the effective value disallows (a `manual` entry is exempt, being typed rather than tracked), and `stopEntriesDisallowedByApps` ends every running entry the setting no longer permits whenever it is saved — from any of the three save paths — so a change takes effect at once rather than at the next start. The hours already tracked are kept: the entry ends where the policy changed. This is not the auto-stopping ADR-0006 and ADR-0022 rejected; theirs is one *timer* silently ending another on a collision, where this is a policy an admin set, the same shape as suspending an Organisation.

Both clients then say so rather than offering a button the server would refuse: the web timer reads `OrganisationProfile.allowedApps` and shows a neutral `InfoMessage` with Start disabled, and the agent does the same from its capture-settings poll — also disabling its project rows, each of which *is* a start button. The agent additionally raises the alarm, since a stopped timer behind other windows is one somebody keeps working past: a desktop notification (the only part that survives a window manager refusing a background app focus, the normal case on Linux), a brief always-on-top pass, and a flashing taskbar entry — all withdrawn once the window has been looked at, since the notification shelf is what carries the unread badge.
_Avoid_: reading `mobile_only` as a working configuration — there is no mobile app, so it leaves a member with no usable timer at all. It exists because the setting mirrors Hubstaff's three-way control; the settings copy says so plainly.

**Work time limits** (Organisation):
What a member's week is expected to look like, and the ceilings on what they may track — five values, each overridable per Membership and each inheriting independently, so someone can take a daily limit of their own and the rest from the Organisation. **Expected work days** (default Mon–Fri) and **Expected weekly hours** are *targets*: reports compare against them, nothing blocks on them. **Tracking disabled days**, the **weekly limit** and the **daily limit** are the only three that cap anything.

Enforced twice. `TimeEntriesService.start` refuses a timer on a disabled day, or once a ceiling is already reached. Both `currentForAccount` (web) and `current` (agent) — which the clients poll constantly — **end a running timer** the moment it crosses one, and report `limitReached`. Reported for a short window after the stop rather than on the stopping read alone: the web and the agent poll independently, so the client that does the stopping is not necessarily the one the person is looking at — the first version let whichever polled first swallow the signal. Each client shows it once per stop. The agent additionally raises and focuses its window and posts a desktop notification, the same escalation **Allowed apps** uses: a timer that stops behind other windows is one somebody keeps working past. Hubstaff does not auto-stop, only blocking the next start; stopping is a deliberate divergence, asked for by the ticket's own edge case. Day boundaries are read in the Organisation's timezone and the weekly reset follows its **Week start**, so a limit means the same thing to everyone in it; worked time is a *union* across Devices, not a sum (see **Worked time**). A day turning disabled leaves time already tracked on it untouched — only new timers are refused.
_Avoid_: reading the hour columns straight off a Membership — pg returns `numeric` as a string, and `undefined` from a partial select becomes `NaN`, which silently compares false against every limit. Go through the resolver.

**Today's totals** (desktop agent):
The per-project figures the agent shows for the current day. Reconciled against the server on every status poll, but **additively**: only completed entries whose id the agent has not already counted are folded in. Replacing them would re-derive the baseline the live tick counts up from, so any disagreement between the server's clock and the machine's would show as the timer jumping backwards — which is why `setRunning` keeps a stopped entry's time *as this device counted it* and records its id as counted. An entry arriving without an id is skipped: there is no telling it from time already counted, and double-counting is worse than a delay.
_Avoid_: re-reading the totals wholesale on a poll — the "timer runs on the local clock" specs guard exactly that. Time added from the web console reaches the agent through this merge, not through a refresh.

**Screenshot frequency** (`settings.screenshotFrequencySeconds`):
How often the agent captures, chosen as a count per ten minutes — `None`, 1, 2 or 3 — the Organisation default with a per-Membership override on `Membership.screenshotFrequencySeconds` (`null` inherits), cleared in bulk by "Apply to all" like **Track apps & URLs**. The per-ten-minutes unit is the settings UI's only; what is stored and what the agent reads are plain seconds, and `perPeriodFromSeconds` rounds an interval the picker doesn't name to the nearest it does.

**`0` is the member-level "None"** — the one value an Organisation cannot hold, since `screenshotsEnabled` is its own off switch. `getCaptureSettings` collapses the two into one number before applying the override, so a member can be switched off while the Organisation captures, or captured while the Organisation default is off; `screenshotsEnabled` is then derived from whether that number is above zero. The agent needs no change for any of it: it already reads both fields, and they now arrive resolved.
_Avoid_: reading `screenshotFrequencySeconds` off the Organisation as what a given person gets — only `getCaptureSettings` knows, and only when given a `userId`.

**Window tracking setting** (Organisation):
An org-level on/off switch, `windowTrackingEnabled` (boolean, defaults `true`), deciding only whether captured activity carries **titles** — an app event the active window's, a URL session its page's — not whether the sample is taken at all, which is **Track apps & URLs**'s job. With it off the agent still records which app was in use and which URL, with `title` null; `AppEventsService.recordBatch` and `UrlSessionsService.sync` strip titles again server-side, so a stale agent can't store one either. Lives on `Organisation.settings` (the same jsonb column `address`/`website`/`idleHandling` already use), editable only by an org admin via the existing organisation-settings update. The desktop agent polls `GET /device/capture-settings` to pick up changes — there's no push channel — and re-checks it immediately on login/logout as well, so a signed-out device never samples. This setting being on isn't sufficient on its own: the sampling loop only actually runs while a Time entry is also currently running for that Membership (Hubstaff-style — tracking follows being clocked in, not merely being logged in), independently polled via the same `/device/time-entries/current` a running timer already uses elsewhere.
_Avoid_: assuming this setting alone is enough to start sampling — see the running-timer condition above. Also avoid assuming Linux window tracking has a single source — see **Window source** below for which applications each one can and cannot see.

**Window source** (Linux):
Where the desktop agent learns which window has focus, and therefore which applications window tracking can see at all. On Wayland it reads X11 first — covering every XWayland client, which is most applications — and falls back to AT-SPI2 (`GnomeWindowFocusTracker`) for Wayland-native windows, which X11 reports as no active window. The two are complementary and neither sees everything: AT-SPI only sees applications publishing an accessibility tree, so JetBrains IDEs (Java/Swing, no Access Bridge) are invisible to it; X11 cannot see Wayland-native windows such as GNOME's own apps. macOS and Windows have no such split — their native APIs see every window.
_Avoid_: concluding an application cannot be tracked because it exposes no accessibility information — that rules out only the AT-SPI half. Also avoid reading the window source off `@paymoapp/active-window`: that addon segfaults inside Electron on Wayland and needs glibc 2.38, so X11 is read as a protocol instead (`X11WindowTracker`).

**App event** / **App activity**:
A single foreground-app/window sample (`AppEvent` entity, `app_events` table) uploaded by the desktop agent's window tracking, batched every 60s (`POST /device/app-events`) — see the Window tracking setting entry above for what gates whether sampling happens at all. Attributed to the Membership signed in on the Device at sample time (same rule as Time entry). No raw-event read API exists — only an aggregated "time spent per app" summary for a given day (`GET .../app-events/summary/mine` for the caller's own, `GET .../memberships/:membershipId/app-events/summary` for admin/manager viewing someone else's, same visibility rule as Activity/Time entry), shown in the web console's Activity → Apps tab. "Time spent" is an approximation — each sample is assumed to represent a fixed 5s of foreground time, not a measured duration.
_Avoid_: confusing this with the **Activity** entity above (user-authored "what I'm working on" notes) — they're unrelated concepts that happen to share a nav section (Activity → Notes vs. Activity → Apps) because that's how this ticket was scoped, not because they're the same thing.

**Screenshot setting** (Organisation):
An org-level on/off switch plus two numbers — `screenshotsEnabled` (boolean, defaults `false`), `screenshotFrequencySeconds` (defaults 600 — 10 min), and `screenshotRetentionDays` (defaults 30) — gating and configuring the desktop agent's screenshot capture. Lives on `Organisation.settings` alongside `windowTrackingEnabled`/`idleHandling`, same mechanism and same `GET /device/capture-settings` endpoint the desktop agent already polled for window tracking (see docs/adr/0012-...). Same running-timer condition as Window tracking setting — `screenshotsEnabled` alone isn't sufficient, capture only runs while a Time entry is also currently running for that Membership.
_Avoid_: assuming a change to `screenshotRetentionDays` is retroactive — it only affects screenshots captured after the change (see Screenshot entry below).

**Screenshot**:
A single captured image of a Membership's primary display (`Screenshot` entity, `screenshots` table), uploaded by the desktop agent's screenshot capture — see the Screenshot setting entry above for what gates and paces it. The image bytes never touch the API: the agent requests a presigned URL and uploads the JPEG straight to Wasabi (S3-compatible object storage), then the agent POSTs only the resulting `storageKey` and capture time as metadata (docs/TECH_DESIGN.md §1/§4's "direct-to-storage" design). Attributed to the Membership signed in on the Device at capture time (same rule as AppEvent/Time entry). Each row's `keptUntil` is fixed at capture time from the org's `screenshotRetentionDays` *as it stood then* — an hourly cleanup job deletes both the row and the underlying object once `keptUntil` passes. No raw browsing API beyond per-day listings, same shape as App event (`GET .../screenshots/mine` for the caller's own, `GET .../memberships/:membershipId/screenshots` for admin/manager viewing someone else's, same visibility rule), shown in the web console's Activity → Screenshots tab; each listed screenshot carries its own presigned, time-limited *viewing* URL, never a permanent link.
_Avoid_: assuming lowering retention deletes existing screenshots sooner — see Screenshot setting above. Also avoid assuming a screenshot can only disappear on a schedule — see **Screenshot deletion** below.

**Screenshot deletion** (`settings.membersCanDeleteScreenshots`):
Who may remove a captured image by hand. An **admin always deletes anyone's**, whatever the setting says. Everyone else deletes only *their own*, and only while the Organisation allows it — an Organisation default with a per-Membership override on `Membership.canDeleteScreenshots` (`null` inherits), the same shape as **Screenshot frequency**. Finance never deletes: the screenshots routes are closed to that role outright, so the Organisation profile's resolved `canDeleteOwnScreenshots` reports `false` for it rather than offering a control that could only be refused. A delete removes the row *and* the Wasabi object, but leaves the Time entry it sat inside alone — the tracked time stays on the timesheet. That is the reverse of **Capture pruning**, where deleting the *entry* is what takes its captures with it.
_Avoid_: reading `membersCanDeleteScreenshots` as what a given person gets — `canDeleteOwnScreenshots` is the resolved answer, and it already folds in role and override. Also avoid reaching for a bulk endpoint: there isn't one, so the console's multi-select issues one DELETE per screenshot and a batch can be *partly* applied, reporting a count rather than an all-or-nothing result.

**Activity tracking setting** (Organisation):
An org-level on/off switch, `activityTrackingEnabled` (boolean, defaults `false`), gating the desktop agent's local keyboard/mouse input-monitoring loop (see docs/adr/0013-...). Lives on `Organisation.settings` alongside `windowTrackingEnabled`/`screenshotsEnabled`, same `GET /device/capture-settings` mechanism and same running-timer condition (tracking follows being clocked in, not merely being logged in). Kept as its own switch rather than folded into `windowTrackingEnabled` — input monitoring is a materially more sensitive capability than reading foreground window titles, and an org may want one without the other.
_Avoid_: assuming this setting alone is enough to start sampling — see the running-timer condition on Window tracking setting above, which applies identically here.

**Activity level** / **ActivitySample**:
A desktop agent's measure of how busy a 10-minute period of tracked time was (`ActivitySample` entity, `activity_samples` table), using Hubstaff's published method (https://support.hubstaff.com/how-are-activity-levels-calculated/): the period is divided into sixty 10-second segments, and `activePercentage` is `activeSegments / 60 * 100`. **"Sample" here means a measurement taken at an interval** — as in `AppEvent`'s `sampledAt`, and the agent's WindowSampler/ScreenshotSampler — never example or test data.

A segment counts as active when the agent's **idle reading** is below the segment length, meaning the last input landed inside it. The original implementation read a global input hook (`uiohook-napi`, see docs/adr/0013-...); deriving it from idle instead needs no native module and records strictly less — how long since the last input, never that input occurred, nor which.

Attributed to the Membership signed in on the Device at sample time (same rule as AppEvent/Screenshot/Time entry). Ingest-only (`POST /device/activity-samples`) — there is no raw-sample read API. The web console reads it only through **Time entry**, which averages the samples covering an entry, weighted by how much of each period falls inside it, and exposes that as the timesheet's Activity column and activity-level filter. An entry no sample covers reports null rather than 0%: "nothing was measured" is a different claim from "they did nothing".
_Avoid_: reading "sample" as seeded or example data — and note the sampling loop only runs while window tracking is on *and* a timer is running, so activity describes tracked time only.
_Avoid_: confusing this with the **Activity** entity above (user-authored notes) or **App event** (foreground window/app samples) — all three are unrelated concepts that happen to share the Activity nav section. Also avoid assuming every screenshot has a matching ActivitySample — activity tracking is a separate on/off switch from screenshots, and the two capture loops aren't guaranteed to phase-align.

**Disclosure region** (`Membership.disclosureRegion`):
An optional jurisdiction code (e.g. country) admin-set on a Membership, used solely to pick which DisclosureVersion (below) applies to that person. Unset means the org's default, region-less disclosure text applies. Not self-reported at signup — an admin assigns/edits it, the same access level as editing a Membership's role.
_Avoid_: "Region" alone — this codebase already has an unrelated **Organisation region** (above); always say "Disclosure region" for this one. Also avoid assuming this is inferred from IP/browser locale, or that it affects anything besides disclosure-text selection.

**DisclosureVersion** (Organisation):
An immutable, published snapshot of the disclosure text shown to a Device's user as a passive notice while capture (window tracking, screenshots, activity sampling) is running on it, scoped to an Organisation and an optional Disclosure region — see docs/adr/0015-... (supersedes docs/adr/0014-...): capture is never blocked on whether this has been shown or acknowledged. An admin can freely edit a draft in place with no version bump; a separate, explicit publish action is what creates the immutable, numbered DisclosureVersion row, which the desktop agent then starts showing in its notice banner. An existing published version's text is never edited in place, since a Consent record (below) points at a specific version and must keep meaning what it meant at the time it was recorded. Resolution for a given Membership only ever considers published versions: the newest one matching their Disclosure region, falling back to the org's newest region-less published version if none matches.
_Avoid_: treating this as a single mutable text field on `Organisation.settings` alongside `idleHandling`/`windowTrackingEnabled`/etc — those are simple toggles with no history; disclosure text needs its own versioned, auditable entity. Also avoid assuming publishing this gates or pauses capture — see docs/adr/0015-....

**Consent record**:
A per-Device acknowledgment that its user was shown a specific DisclosureVersion and chose to click through it, recording who (the Membership), which Device, which DisclosureVersion, and when. Consent is per Device, not per Membership — someone with two Devices acknowledges separately on each, since the notice is shown locally on that specific machine. Purely an audit trail (see docs/adr/0015-...): there is no "declining," and neither answer changes whether capture runs.
_Avoid_: assuming this gates device use or capture in any way — as of docs/adr/0015-... it's informational record-keeping only, a change from the hard-gate design docs/adr/0014-... originally shipped with.
